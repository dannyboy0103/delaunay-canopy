TL;DR

Delaunay Canopy scores a Delaunay graph with geometric priors to reconstruct building wireframes precisely, even in sparse, noisy, and internal-corner regions.

Delaunay Graph Scoring

Delaunay triangulation quantifies local curvature, transforming the underlying surface geometry into essential curvature signatures.

Adaptive Search Space

Corner and wire selection leverages the geometric cue to adaptively adjust the search space, focusing the model on probable candidates.

State of the Art

State-of-the-art results on the Building3D Tallinn city and entry-level datasets, supported by a comprehensive experimental study.

Overview

From point cloud to wireframe

  1. 01 Delaunay Graph Scoring. Delaunay triangulation approximates the underlying surface of the point cloud, then region-wise curvature metrics are computed for each element in the order of face, edge, and vertex.
  2. 02 Corner Selection. Leveraging the vertex-wise prior, corner score sampling isolates a small, geometry-aware subset of points likely to represent true corners, and the corner selection module refines them into the final corners.
  3. 03 Wire Selection. Leveraging the edge-wise prior, each wire candidate takes a path score averaged along its shortest Delaunay path, and the wire selection module uses that score to determine valid wires.
Delaunay Graph Scoring

Curvature signatures from geometry

We apply Delaunay triangulation to the airborne LiDAR building point cloud to approximate its underlying surface. This process generates a Delaunay graph \(G = (V, E, F)\), and geometric priors for curvature characterization are assigned to its faces, edges, and vertices in turn.

Face Normal Computation

For every face within the Delaunay graph, the normal vector is computed from its three constituent vertices.

Edge-Wise Dihedral Angle

For every edge, the dihedral angle is the angle between the normal vectors of its two adjacent faces. A large dihedral angle signifies a sharp fold or corner where the two adjacent faces meet.

Vertex-Wise Corner Score

For every vertex, the corner score is determined by aggregating the dihedral angles of all its incident edges into their mean. A high corner score suggests that the vertex is highly likely to be an actual corner of the building.

Corner Score Sampling

Sampling where corners live

Each panel runs from the raw input point cloud down to 500 points and then to 150 points, with the ground truth wireframe kept faintly visible throughout. While farthest point sampling spreads its budget evenly over the surface, corner score sampling retains points located in areas of pronounced curvature (roof corners and edges), making them highly probable corner candidates.

Wire-Wise Path Score

Curvature along the shortest path

For a wire candidate \(w_{ij}\) connecting vertices \(v_i\) and \(v_j\), we first find the shortest path \(P^{G}_{i \to j}\) between these two vertices on the Delaunay graph. The edges along that path share the same local region as the wire candidate, so averaging their dihedral angles gives a path score that represents the wire curvature.

$$S_P(v_i, v_j) = \frac{1}{|L(P^{G}_{i \to j})|}\sum_{e \in L(P^{G}_{i \to j})} \theta_e.$$

Through prior-based query scaling, each wire query is modulated by \(s^{w}=\sigma(S^{w}_{P})\), damping low-score wires and emphasizing high-confidence ones. The model's attention is focused on geometrically plausible structures, leading to stable and accurate wire predictions.
